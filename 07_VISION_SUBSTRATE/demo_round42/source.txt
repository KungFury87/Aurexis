The oldest unsolved problem in cognitive science is not how minds are made. It is how meaning is. How does a thing in the head, or a thing in the silicon, come to be about the world it inhabits — not merely affected by it, not merely correlated with it, but referring to it, the way a word refers, the way a sentence is true or false?

This essay proposes that the bridge has not been built because there is no bridge to build. The grounding problem is an artifact of starting from the wrong side. If we start from measurements, and let meaning emerge from their composition, the bridge is unnecessary.
